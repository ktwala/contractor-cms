## Three experiences

### 1. Operations Console — *Managing an External Workforce*

**Audience:** Procurement, operations, HR operations, security, governance (MTN).

**Question:** *How do we govern an external workforce?*

**Story:**

```text
Trust supplier → Accept worker → Manage workforce → Manage engagement → React
```

Overview is **operational work** — today's attention, not a navigation menu.

---

### 2. Supplier Portal — *Working as a Supplier*

**Audience:** Supplier companies.

**Question:** *How do I work with my customer?*

**Story:**

```text
Maintain my organisation → Introduce workers → Track nominations → Submit timesheets → Track invoices
```

Suppliers never think about workforce administration. They think: *Did my worker get approved?*

Overview is an **inbox** — welcome, today's attention, quick actions.

---

### 3. Executive Architecture — *Platform Architecture*

**Audience:** MTN executives, enterprise architects.

**Question:** *Where should the truth live?*

**Story:** Five minutes **without logging in** — department questions, one-owner principle — then show EWP. Architecture is revealed **only at the close**.

See [`DEMO-PLATFORM-ARCHITECTURE.md`](./DEMO-PLATFORM-ARCHITECTURE.md).

---

## Narrative discipline (all demos)

| During the demo | At the close only |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Audience language — *Operations accepted responsibility* | Truth cascade |
| Business decisions and outcomes | One owner per truth |
| What requires attention today | Why each step behaved that way |

Do **not** end every act with architecture jargon (*Workforce owns workforce truth*). The audience understands responsibility; save the design for the final five minutes.
